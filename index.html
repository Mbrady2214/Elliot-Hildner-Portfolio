About Me

A passion for design and a love of sports.

My passion for design and my love of sports led me to a career that puts the two together. I earned my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering at Oregon State University in 2025, and I'm now working on my M.S. in Sports Product Design at the University of Oregon in Portland.

Most of my projects start with one specific problem. A golfer's gloves soaking through between shots. A roundnet player losing grip on a hard cut. These are the types of issues every athlete has come across. These are the types of issues I use new designs to fix.

I build and test my own prototypes, and when I'm not in the studio I'm usually in the shop turning baseball bats on a lathe.

“Creating an accurate testing method takes longer than the actual testing.” My SpikeShift reflection

The Playbook

Five plays I run on every project, from the first question about the athlete to the last test.

  1. Play 01

    Research

    Find the athlete's real problem. Published biomechanics, competitor breakdowns, brand research and feedback from the people who use the gear.

    Clover research
  2. Play 02

    Sketch

    Get the ideas out fast and wide. The recovery sleeve went through more than 50 sketches before a direction stuck.

    Sleeve sketches
  3. Play 03

    Model

    Surfaces and SubD in Rhino, then 1:2 drawings with sections and dimensions so the part can actually be made.

    Lotus I CAD
  4. Play 04

    Build

    Cardboard and tape, then wood and leather, then printed TPU. Real materials on a real body as early as possible.

    Rooster builds
  5. Play 05

    Test

    Force gauges, faucet tests, wear tests and feedback rounds. Then change the design and run it again.

    Traction test

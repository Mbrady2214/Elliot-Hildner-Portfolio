01

Brief

A cleat for a sport that doesn't have one.

At a glanceGoal · Approach · Result

Goal

More grip for roundnet's multi-directional moves without locking the foot in a pivot.

Approach

Three stud shapes placed by movement, modeled in Rhino and printed as PETG traction slabs.

Result

+9.2% medial and +11.5% lateral slip resistance over a lacrosse stud layout.

02

Research

Roundnet-specific movements and plantar pressure.

  1. Pressure moves around the foot depending on the movement, so the stud layout should change by zone.
  2. The moves that decide roundnet points are the medial push-off, the lateral landing and the forward jump.
  3. More traction isn't automatically better. Too much grip can keep the foot from releasing in a pivot.

05

Traction Test

Baseline lacrosse layout vs. SpikeShift.

I printed a section of each outsole in PETG, loaded it with a 10 lb dumbbell on turf and pulled it with a force gauge until it slipped. Three pulls per side for each slab, twelve in all.

  • SpikeShift held better than the baseline in both directions, with the biggest gain laterally.
  • Next step: full-shoe testing on players for comfort, maneuverability and consistency.
